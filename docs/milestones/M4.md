## Methodology

Notation: E_t is equity at day t's close, C = ₹5,00,000, and N is the number of trading days.

**Returns and growth**
- **ROI** = (E_last − C) / C.
- **CAGR** = (E_last / C)^(365.25 / calendar days) − 1. Here that's 1,841 days, about 5.04 years. The strategy doesn't compound; this only annualises the curve.

**Drawdown**
- The running peak starts at C.
- Max drawdown in ₹ = max(peak − E_t); in % = max((peak − E_t) / peak).
- Each is reported with its own peak, trough and recovery dates.
- It's measured on the **daily mark-to-market** curve, so drawdowns inside a trade count.

**Return on notional (the headline Sharpe/Sortino basis)**
- r_t = Σ over open trades of (that day's change in net P&L per unit) / (NIFTY close at that trade's entry).
- Net P&L per unit = net ₹ mark / quantity, so slippage and charges are included.
- On flat days r_t = 0. The daily values sum exactly to Σ(trade net points / entry level), which is one of the reconciliation checks.

**₹ returns**
- r_t = E_t / E_(t−1) − 1, where the first day is measured against C.

**Sharpe and Sortino**
- Sharpe = mean(r − target) / sd(r − target) × √252, with the sample standard deviation.
- Sortino = mean(r − target) / √(mean(min(r − target, 0)²)) × √252, averaging over all days.
- Daily target = (1 + annual)^(1/252) − 1.
- Notional headline: target **0%**. The 6.5% versions are reported only as sensitivities.
- ₹ series and benchmark: target **6.5%**.

**t-statistic**
- t = mean / (s / √n), with s the sample standard deviation over the trades.
- It is computed on **net % of premium** (leading) and **net points per unit**, never on ₹.
- Net % of premium (M3 definition) = 100 × pnl_points_net / premium_fill_points, where pnl_points_net = pnl_rs_net / quantity and premium_fill_points = Σ over legs of (close − slippage).

**Win rate**
- A win is net P&L > 0, a loss < 0; exact zeros are counted separately.

**Benchmark**
- C buys NIFTY 50 TRI at the first day's close and holds it to the last day's close, with no costs or taxes.
- It uses the run's own index file, and the hash of that file is checked against the run's manifest.

**Monthly and yearly tables**
- Each period starts from the previous period's ending equity.
- Trades are grouped by expiry year, the year their P&L is realised.

**Missing data**
- If any daily value is missing, that series' Sharpe and Sortino are returned as **null with the reason**. A return is never stitched across a gap.
- Drawdown counts the days it skipped. ROI and CAGR need only the end points.
- The benchmark is not computed if the TRI is missing on the first or last day; interior gaps stay missing.
- Coverage for this run is complete: 0 missing days in every series.

## Key design decisions

1. **Metrics are computed from the saved run folder, not from the engine.** Any run can be
   re-analysed later with `nifty-backtest metrics`, and every number traces back to files on disk.
2. **D1 (target rates).** A short straddle never invests its notional, so P&L per unit of
   notional is already an excess return. Subtracting 6.5% again would charge interest on money
   that was never spent, and the sensitivity rows show how much that choice matters (0.56 →
   −0.38). The ₹ series does hold real capital, so it uses 6.5%, as does the benchmark.
3. **D3 (t-statistics).** ₹ isn't used because lot sizes changed up to 3×. % of premium leads
   because it is also independent of the index level (17k → 26k), which points per unit are not.
4. **Gaps are null, not stitched.** A two-day return treated as one daily observation would
   understate volatility. The code refuses instead, and a test proves it.
5. **D6 reproducibility.**
   - `config.json` stores the concrete end date.
   - Saving from a dirty tree logs a warning.
   - These reference results come from a clean run at a committed version.
   - The two runs made during M3, before the M3 commit, record commit `421c50b` with
     uncommitted changes, so they are superseded but kept for history.
6. **"By date" and "by expiry" year totals differ slightly** (2024: ₹37,956 vs ₹38,047). A
   trade entered in late Dec 2024 and settled in Jan 2025 contributes mark-to-market to one year
   and realised P&L to the other. Both columns sum to the same total.

## Limitations

- **Capital, not margin.** Returns are on ₹5,00,000 of capital, not on the margin actually used,
  which was lower. Return on margin would be higher; no margin figure is quoted without a
  dated, citable source.
- **Statistical weakness.** At the 5% level the average trade is **not** distinguishable from
  zero: t = 0.78 on % of premium, 1.37 on points.
- **Tail risk.** Losses are larger than wins on average, and the worst trade is −₹41,067.
  Sharpe and Sortino summarise volatility and do not fully capture short-option tail risk.
  Spec-listed metrics only; nothing else was added.
- **The headline ratio rests on D1.** The notional Sharpe uses a 0% target; at 6.5% it turns
  negative.
- **Cost assumptions.** Brokerage (₹20/order), slippage (0.5 point per leg) and the top-slab NSE
  charge before Oct 2024 are assumptions. M7 tests sensitivity to them.
- **No out-of-sample test yet.** That's M7. No taxes are modelled for either the strategy or the
  benchmark (spec v1).
- **Partial years.** 2021 (63 days) and 2026 (196 days) are partial.
- **Context only.** The 2 Mar 2024 VIX value came from NSE's 2-decimal archive file; VIX is used
  for context only, not in any metric.

## Explain-back questions

1. The headline Sharpe is 0.56, but at a 6.5% target it is −0.38. Why does the headline use 0%
   for the notional series but 6.5% for the rupee series?
2. Walk through the notional return on the entry day of a trade. Why is it slightly negative
   even if the options don't move?
3. Why is the t-statistic computed on % of premium and points per unit but not on rupees? What
   does t = 0.78 tell you?
4. The max drawdown is ₹1,12,676.73 (22.26%). Why is it measured on the daily mark-to-market
   curve instead of on closed trades only, and what would the closed-trade version miss?
5. If one day's equity were missing, what happens to Sharpe, Sortino, ROI and drawdown, and why
   not just compute a two-day return across the gap?
