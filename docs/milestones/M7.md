## The question and the answer

**Question:** does the NIFTY weekly short straddle have a robust edge after costs, and does
any single pre-registered condition improve it?

**Answer, for the previously observed data (Oct 2021 to Oct 2026):**
- **No edge is demonstrated.** The baseline's mean net result is +2.69% of premium per trade in
  development and +7.11% in validation, but both 95% intervals include zero.
- **No condition is shown to help.** All six pre-registered variants are *inconclusive*: none
  reaches a Holm-adjusted p below 0.05.
- **Nothing is confirmed.** Only the forward holdout could confirm a result, and no variant
  qualified to be taken there.

This is a legitimate and useful result: with ~150 trades, the data cannot separate these
effects from noise, and the study says so instead of picking the best-looking setting.

## How the study was run

1. **Pre-registration first.** [`docs/research/m7_preregistration.md`](../research/m7_preregistration.md)
   and [`docs/research/m7_plan.toml`](../research/m7_plan.toml) fixed the periods, variants,
   settings, statistics and decision rules. They were committed as `40a0aea` before any
   variant was run on real data. `nifty-backtest study` refuses to run if either file has
   uncommitted changes, and records both files' SHA-256 and commit in `study.json`.
2. **The study ran once from that commit** (`20261027-005848-m7-short-straddle-robustness`).
3. **Validation of the outputs** (below), then the study API and research page.
4. **Reproduction:** the study was re-run from the final code commit
   `6a49d7a` (`20261027-022229-m7-short-straddle-robustness`, no uncommitted changes). Every
   statistic, verdict and all 72 runs' trade logs matched the first run byte for byte; the only
   differences are the two presentation fixes listed under "Corrections" (10 plateau flags in
   the B0-centred family became n/a).

**Periods:**

| Period | Dates | Baseline trades | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Development | 1 Oct 2021 – 30 Sep 2024 | 156 | verdicts are decided here |
| Historical validation | 1 Oct 2024 – 16 Oct 2026 | 106 | **previously observed** in M4: can show a result does not hold up, cannot confirm one |
| Forward holdout | entries from 17 Oct 2026 | — | evaluated **once**, at 52 completed baseline trades or on 31 Jan 2028, whichever first |

The one trade that spans the boundary (entered 27 Sep 2024, expiring 3 Oct 2024) belongs to
neither period.

## Results

### Does the baseline have an edge? (net % of premium per trade)

| Period | Trades | Mean | t | 95% CI, i.i.d. | 95% CI, blocks of 4 | Lag-1 autocorr. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Development | 156 | +2.69% | 0.47 | [−8.46, +13.46] | [−8.20, +13.18] | −0.05 |
| Validation | 106 | +7.11% | 1.05 | [−6.39, +20.12] | [−4.42, +18.45] | −0.24 |

**Verdict:** edge not demonstrated (both envelopes include zero).

### The six pre-registered conditions

Paired with the baseline expiry by expiry, in % of the baseline's premium; a skipped week counts
as 0 points. "Envelope" is the conservative envelope of the i.i.d. and block intervals.

| Variant | Change | Dev mean diff | Dev raw p | Dev Holm p | Dev envelope | Dev max DD (B0 22.26%) | Validation mean diff | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1a | skip if VIX(t−1) < 13.37 | +2.43% | 0.535 | 1.000 | [−4.93, +10.31] | 22.26% | −3.75% | inconclusive |
| V1b | skip if VIX(t−1) ≥ 16.88 | −1.03% | 0.729 | 1.000 | [−8.04, +5.76] | 12.47% | −4.72% | inconclusive |
| V2 | skip knowable scheduled-event weeks | −3.74% | 0.038 | 0.191 | [−7.68, −0.09] | 19.93% | −0.20% | inconclusive |
| V3 | stop-loss at 2× premium per leg | +1.87% | 0.741 | 1.000 | [−8.87, +12.93] | 8.78% | +0.46% | inconclusive |
| V4 | take profit at 50% decay | −7.35% | 0.017 | 0.100 | [−13.16, −1.05] | 28.48% | −5.83% | inconclusive |
| V5 | skip if ADX(14, t−1) > 25 | −3.98% | 0.206 | 0.822 | [−10.02, +2.27] | 19.21% | +0.27% | inconclusive |

**How to read it:**
- **V2 and V4 look worse, but are not declared worse.** Their envelopes lie below zero, yet
  after correcting for six tests (Holm) neither p-value is below 0.05. The pre-registered rule
  uses the Holm p-value, so both are inconclusive. Skipping event weeks (V2) removed 15
  development weeks that had averaged +38.9% of premium; in validation, the same kind of weeks
  averaged +3.1% and V2 made no difference.
- **V3's stop-loss cut the drawdown** (8.78% against 22.26%) and the worst-5% trades
  (−95% against −174% of premium), without a measurable change in the mean. That is a risk
  observation, not a verdict: the rule requires a significant mean improvement first.
- **The VIX filters (V1a, V1a-p25, V1a-p50) and V5-30 have exactly B0's development drawdown.**
  This was checked, not assumed: the drawdown ran from 12 Oct 2021 to 17 May 2022, a high-VIX
  stretch, and all 33 trades in it are identical in those variants. Skipping *low*-VIX weeks
  cannot avoid a high-VIX loss streak.

### Realism checks and sensitivity (reported, never used to choose)

- **B0-R (strike from the previous close):** development +0.63% vs B0, validation −1.42%; both
  intervals include zero. Choosing the strike from the same close that fills the trade makes
  no measurable difference.
- **Slippage:** B0-R and V3 at 1 and 2 points per leg move the mean differences by at most
  2.3 points of premium; every interval still includes zero.
- **Families:** the V3 stop multiple (1.5–3×) and the V4 take-profit level (30–70%) keep the
  same sign across neighbouring settings in both periods. The VIX (V1a) and ADX (V5)
  thresholds flip sign between neighbours in at least one period, which is what noise looks
  like.
- **Entry timing (E1, E2, E4, E5):** descriptive only; every one of them changes sign between
  development and validation.

### Cost grid (B0)

Mean net % of premium falls smoothly as costs rise: from +3.65% (no slippage, no brokerage) to
+0.76% (2 points per leg, ₹40 per order) in development, and from +7.78% to +5.70% in
validation. No cell has a t-statistic above 1.15. Each ₹20 of brokerage costs ₹47.20 per trade
(two orders plus 18% GST), in both periods.

### Regimes (B0, descriptive)

By VIX(t−1) tercile (development thresholds), the best and worst terciles swap between periods
(development: middle best, low worst; validation: high best, middle worst). There is no stable
regime effect to build a filter on.

## Key design decisions

- **One change per variant, enforced in code.** `load_plan` rejects a variant that differs from
  its parent in more than one setting, so the effect of each condition stands alone.
- **Paired comparison on the baseline's premium.** Comparing week by week removes the market's
  common noise; using the baseline's premium as the denominator keeps a skipped week in the
  sample as a 0, so a filter cannot look better just by trading less.
- **Holm, not just intervals.** Six primaries means six chances to be lucky. The verdict uses
  the Holm-adjusted p-value; the intervals describe size and uncertainty.
- **Two bootstraps, conservative envelope.** The i.i.d. bootstrap assumes independent weeks; the
  circular block bootstrap (blocks of 4, about a month) keeps short runs together. Verdicts use
  the lower of the two lower bounds and the higher of the two upper bounds, so neither
  assumption can make a result look stronger.
- **Previous-close information only.** Every filter uses data available at the previous
  session's close; an event counts only if it was publicly scheduled by then.
- **The API and dashboard compute nothing.** Every statistic and verdict is in `study.json`,
  made by Python; the browser only formats and lays it out. A study's runs are served by the
  same route functions as top-level runs.

Found while validating; neither changes a number or a verdict.
1. `report.md` said "moving blocks"; the implementation (and the pre-registration) is circular
   blocks. The wording is fixed.
2. The entry-timing family is centred on B0 itself, whose difference from itself is 0, so a
   "same sign as neighbours" check has no meaning there. It now reports n/a instead of "no".

Also fixed: the trade page labelled a leg bought back by a stop as "Value at expiry". Trades
with an exit rule now show their exits and "Value at exit". The trade-log column keeps its name
(`*_value_at_expiry`) so the baseline's output stays byte-identical; its meaning for early
exits is documented in the engine and the API schema.

## Limitations

- **Low power.** With 156 and 106 paired weeks, the primaries' development envelopes are 8 to
  22 points of premium wide. Effects of a few points cannot be told apart from noise;
  "inconclusive" means exactly that, not "no effect".
- **Serial dependence.** Lag-1 autocorrelations are small (baseline −0.05 and −0.24); the block
  bootstrap allows for month-long dependence, but not for regimes lasting longer.
- **Validation was seen before.** It can only reject; the forward holdout is the only
  confirmation, and nothing qualified for it.
- **End-of-day data.** Stops and take-profits use daily high/low with conservative fill rules;
  intraday paths, gaps within the day and queue position are not modelled.
- **Costs are assumptions.** Brokerage and slippage are configurable; the grid shows how
  results move with them.
